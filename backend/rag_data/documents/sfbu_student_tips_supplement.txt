DOCUMENT: Practical Tips for New SFBU Students — Things Nobody Tells You

AUTHOR NOTE: This document supplements the official SFBU orientation materials with practical, student-perspective advice for navigating your first weeks at SFBU.

---

BEFORE YOU ARRIVE

1. Set up your SFBU email immediately after being admitted. Many important communications (Canvas access, orientation details, housing lottery) come through your SFBU email. Don't use a personal email — you might miss critical deadlines.

2. Apply for housing as early as possible. On-campus housing is limited and runs on a lottery system (except for Startup Scholars). If you wait, you may not get a spot.

3. Book your flight to SJC (San Jose) if possible — it's the closest airport at only 10 miles from campus. OAK and SFO are cheaper but farther. From SJC, a 15-minute Uber to campus costs around $15-20.

4. Text your Enrollment Counselor when you land so they know to expect you on campus.

---

FIRST WEEK SURVIVAL

5. Your student ID takes 1-2 weeks. You'll get your photo taken at orientation but won't have the physical card right away. Use your enrollment confirmation email as a temporary ID if needed.

6. Pick up your AC Transit EasyPass from the front desk of the Main Building within your first week. This is your FREE unlimited bus pass — don't forget to get it. Contact Michael Leinhos for questions.

7. Canvas is your academic hub. All assignments, grades, syllabi, and professor communications go through Canvas. Set up notifications immediately so you don't miss anything.

8. The Add/Drop period is only about one week (e.g., August 18–23). If you want to change your schedule, do it immediately at the start of the semester. After that window closes, changes are much harder.

9. Go to orientation even if you think you don't need it. You'll get stamps in your booklet, meet key staff, and learn things that aren't on the website.

---

ACADEMICS

10. SFBU classes are small — usually under 30 students. This means professors notice when you're not there and when you're not engaged. Attendance really matters here more than at large universities.

11. Talk to your professor during office hours. Because class sizes are small, professors are accessible. One good conversation during office hours can change how they see you — and can help a lot when you're struggling.

12. Use Alma (SFBU's AI teaching assistant inside Canvas) for help with Gen-Ed coursework. It's trained on actual course materials, not just general knowledge.

13. If you're struggling with math or English specifically, see Evelyn Binoya (Math) or Leslie Ji (English) in the SSH. Both offer tutoring, study sessions, and workshops. Don't wait until you're failing — go early.

14. Academic advising is your friend. Rebecca (undergraduate) or Dr. Mariam Ghazvini (graduate) can help you plan your courses, avoid graduation mistakes, and understand your program requirements.

---

FINANCES & BANKING

15. Bank of America offers no-fee student checking accounts and has a location 0.4 miles from campus. PNC Bank does not require a driver's license — good for international students who don't yet have a state ID.

16. Sometimes SFBU hosts on-campus bank account opening events. Watch for announcements — these make opening an account much easier.

17. For textbooks, use Chegg to rent instead of buy. You can save up to 90%. Also check if your professors will accept an older edition — often they will.

18. Student DashPass is $4.99/month if you use DoorDash often. Microsoft Office 365 is free or nearly free through your student email.

---

MENTAL HEALTH & WELLNESS

19. TimelyCare gives you 12 FREE counseling sessions per year, 24/7 virtual access. Sign up with your SFBU email at timely.care. Many students don't know about this and pay out of pocket when they don't have to.

20. The Let'sTALK program trains students and staff as peer support people. You can identify them by a pink bracelet or Let'sTALK pin. They're not therapists but they will listen without judgment.

21. Weekly Wellness Workshops happen every Tuesday through Student Wellness Services. Activities include yoga, Zumba, pickleball, and meditation. These are free and a great way to decompress midweek.

22. The Food Pantry and Clothing Closet in the SSH are available to any student who needs them. There's no shame in using them — that's exactly what they're there for.

---

SOCIAL LIFE

23. The LRC (Learning Resource Center) on the ground floor of the Main Building is where most student social life happens. If you want to meet people, just go there and sit down. People will talk to you.

24. Join at least one club in your first month. It doesn't matter which one — the point is to have a consistent reason to see the same people every week. That's how friendships form.

25. SFBU is a very international campus. Don't only hang out with people from your home country. Some of the best connections you'll make are with people from completely different backgrounds.

26. Because SFBU is small, word travels fast. Be respectful to everyone — the student you're rude to today might be in three of your classes next semester.

---

TRANSPORTATION TIPS

27. The Warm Springs/South Fremont BART station is the closest BART stop to campus. From there you can reach Oakland in ~30 min and downtown San Francisco in ~60 min.

28. Rideshare (Uber/Lyft) from campus to the BART station is usually $8-12. Budget for this if you plan to explore the Bay Area on weekends.

29. Lyft offers student discounts — check your student email for a promo code or use Student Beans to find deals.
